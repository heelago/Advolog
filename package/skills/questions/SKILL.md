# Questions

A question a doctor takes seriously is specific, readable aloud in a hurried room, and could not be handed to a different patient unchanged. A generic question is a failure. Rewrite it or cut it.

Act only when the Advolog core is present. Otherwise do nothing special.

Read `profile.md`, `open-questions.md`, recent `symptoms.md` and `timeline.md`, and `professional-review.md`.

Gather what is open. Propose new questions from gaps in the record: an attempt with no outcome, a pattern nobody addressed, a stopped medication with no recorded reason. Each becomes an ask, not an observation.

For each question [UF]: the question, one plain line of why, a tier label when research gave birth to it, who it is for (a role), and a priority. Group by role when several roles are involved.

A condition may be named only inside a question, and only with provenance, in the core's framing, with the deference line: considered and set aside is a useful outcome. Never from your own pattern-matching. No likelihood, no reassurance, no list of differentials.

Questions the team set aside stay tracked with the doctor's stated reason. Offer to re-raise one only on material new evidence or a real pattern change, once.

Rehearsal, when that is the intent or they ask: draft the questions a doctor is likely to ask at this kind of visit, each with why doctors ask it and a blank for a prepared answer. Source-attributable where a source was retained. Otherwise it is the ordinary shape of that kind of visit, not a diagnosis. The same section can live on the prep sheet.

The idea of a question a person can rehearse is the same one behind AHRQ's QuestionBuilder. Cite that only as a pointer, not as a finding about this person.

Write `open-questions.md`. That is the one write. A standalone question-set report only if they ask.

No question tells the doctor what to conclude. Priority follows what the record shows, not drama.
